Safety Guidelines

Candle safety instructions

Candles are beautiful, safe products when used correctly. The BCF works with its members to ensure that every candle sold in the UK meets the highest safety standards. The guidelines below set out the key safety instructions that apply to all candles. Always read and follow the instructions provided on your candle's packaging.

Never Leave a Burning Candle Unattended

Always put out candles when leaving a room or before going to sleep. You should also put out a candle if the candle smokes, repeatedly flickers or the flame is not controlled.

Keep Out of Reach of Children and Pets

Never place lit candles where they can be easily knocked over by anyone, especially children and pets.

Keep Away from Anything That Can Catch Fire

Place candles on a heat resistant and non-flammable surface, at least 30cm away from anything that can catch fire, including but not limited to curtains and decorations.

Leave at Least 7cm Between Burning Candles

Follow the recommended minimum distance on the packaging, or if not available, leave at least 7cm to be safe. Candles placed too close together can drip or soot, and tealights can flare up.

Do Not Place Candles in a Draught

Keep candles out of draughts to prevent rapid, uneven burning, sooting and excessive dripping. Lightweight curtains can waft into the flame if in a draught.

Do Not Place Candles Near a Source of Heat

The higher the temperature around a candle, the greater the risks. For example, tealights on a TV can flare up and melt through the plastic, and other types of candles might drip wax.

Always Use a Candleholder

Candle holders should be heat resistant and non-flammable. They should also be stable and big enough to collect dripping wax.

Place Candles in an Upright Position

Stand candles straight to avoid dripping and minimise the risk of flaring. Glass cups can crack if the flame touches the side.

Trim Wick to About 1cm Before Lighting

Trim the wick with scissors or a wick trimmer before lighting. If the wick is too long, a lump of carbon might form on it as it burns, known as clubbing, or the flame might become too high and start to soot.

Trim Edges to a Height of 1cm

If your candle was not produced in a glass form, trim the edges with a knife to a height of 1cm before burning. This ensures proper airflow to the flame.

Keep the Wax Pool Clear of Debris

Flammable objects in the wax pool, such as matches, insects, flammable decorations or lumps from the wick, can ignite and cause the candle to overheat and flare up.

Do Not Move a Burning Candle

Moving a candle can have the same effect as placing it in a draught, causing sooting and dripping. If a tealight is moving when all the wax is molten, the wick might change position and cause the candle to flare up.

Always Snuff Out the Flame. Do Not Blow It Out.

Use a candle snuffer when putting out a candle. It is especially important not to blow out a candle in a container, such as a tealight, as this increases the risk of flaring and hot wax could spatter.

Never Use Liquid to Extinguish a Flame

If the candle cannot be extinguished easily, cover it with a damp cloth. Never use water to extinguish candles. The water can cause the hot wax to spatter and the candle container to break.

Do Not Allow the Candle to Burn Out Completely

Put out candles when they have burnt down to about 2cm from their holders. If a candle burns down completely it can cause burn marks or even start a fire, as a glass holder can crack and metal holders can become extremely hot.

Maximum Height of Candle

Using a higher candle than recommended in a lantern may cause the candle to melt down due to overheating. This also means the lantern will become very hot and burning accidents may occur.

Only Use Tealights in Holders with Sufficient Ventilation

Only use tealights in holders, oil burners and warming stoves with sufficient ventilation. Insufficient ventilation can cause the tealight to flare up.

Use a Suitable Container for Liquifying Candles

Always use a candle holder of suitable size and shape, as some candles become liquid while burning. Always use a candle holder of suitable size and shape.

Floating Candles for Use in Water Only

Floating candles must only be used floating in water. Put them in a suitable water-filled bowl.

Good to Know

Additional safety guidance

The First Burn

The first time you burn a new candle is the most important. Allow the wax to melt all the way to the edge of the container before extinguishing it. This prevents tunnelling and ensures an even burn for the life of the candle.

Burn Time

Do not burn a candle for more than 4 hours at a time. After extinguishing, allow the candle to cool and solidify before relighting. This prevents the wick from becoming too long and the wax pool from becoming too deep.

Storing Candles

Store candles in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and sources of heat. This prevents discolouration and helps the candle maintain its fragrance and shape over time.

Candle Safety Standards

All candles sold in the UK must meet the requirements of BS EN 15493 (candle safety requirements), BS EN 15494 (candle safety labels) and BS EN 15426 (sooting performance testing). BCF members are committed to meeting and exceeding these standards.
